How tracking ships helps you

Your car's journey from Shanghai to Southampton, and when your VIN usually shows up.

  1. ShanghaiCar built and loaded on a ship. That's when it's truly matched to your order.
  2. SingaporeVIN usually appears, but only in the bots and scripts
  3. Cape of Good HopeVIN usually appears in the Tesla app
  4. SouthamptonArrives about 35–45 days after leaving Shanghai
Why follow the ships?

The TMC Shipping Movements thread tracks Tesla shipments heading to Europe throughout 2026.

Once you've ordered, there's usually a quiet spell when you hear very little from Tesla. Tesla builds cars in batches, not to order. A car is only really matched to your order once it's assigned to production and allocated to a ship. Following those ships is the most reliable way to get a feel for when your car will arrive.

Where the cars come from
  • Model Y: most UK and European cars are now built at Giga Berlin, so far fewer arrive by sea from China.
  • Model 3: still built at Giga Shanghai and shipped to the main hubs, including Zeebrugge, Koper and Barcelona.
  • Route: all ships from Shanghai to Europe now go round the Cape of Good Hope to keep schedules reliable.
Shanghai → SouthamptonTypical transit
Via Suez Canal (old route)30–35 days
Via Cape of Good Hope (now)35–45 days

Going round the Cape adds roughly 3,000–4,000 nautical miles, which is usually one to two extra weeks depending on the ship's speed and the sea conditions.

VINs and the trackers

A VIN is usually assigned once your car is built and allocated to a ship, often when it's already at the port or at sea. The VIN is often in Tesla's system days before the app shows it.

  • Trackers: community tools use the Tesla Fleet API to check your order status more often than the app does.
  • Early data: when your order is matched, these tools can show your VIN and updated delivery window before the app refreshes.

See @Temboa's bot/script help post for the tools.

Tracking tools and thanks

Special thanks to @Temboa, who keeps posting updates, tracking and arrival estimates for ships heading to European ports.

  • MarineTraffic is widely used to follow ship positions using VHF and satellite data.
  • @Temboa and friends built Tesla Ships, which tracks confirmed and suspected ships.
  • Many "suspect" ships turn out to be false alarms, so the thread sticks to verified data.
Tracking FAQs

Questions people ask

When are VINs assigned for cars leaving Shanghai?

Usually while the ship is sailing through the South China Sea, before it reaches Singapore or the Jakarta Strait. We've also seen assignments in the Indian Ocean, but that's less common.

When are VINs assigned for Model Y from Berlin?

Model Y VINs are sent directly from the Berlin factory.

What you seeWhat it means
No VIN in the thread's toolsYour car doesn't exist yet
VIN in the tools, not in the appProduction is planned
VIN in the Tesla appCar is built. Now it's down to transport

Model Ys for Ireland go to Zeebrugge, then by ship to Dublin.

It's hard to tell which ship a Berlin-built Model Y is on. They travel in small batches alongside other brands' cars, and several ships may do the run each week.

My EDD keeps moving. Is that normal?

Yes. Until you have a VIN, the Estimated Delivery Date is Tesla's best guess and can jump around a lot. Don't lose hope if it jumps later. It can jump back at any point.

How does sharing my VIN with @Temboa help?

When you share your VIN and EDD, it joins everyone else's to build a picture of which VINs for which countries are on the move. Sometimes one Tesla adviser telling one customer something is enough to confirm it for everyone.

Your VIN isn't secret. Anyone can read it through the windscreen when the car's parked. If you'd rather not share all of it, the last 5 digits are still a real help.

Once I have a VIN, can I track my car on any ship?

Some shipping companies let you track a VIN on a ship, but most don't. You can try your luck with Logisoft's VIN tracker.

Could my car come with the black headliner and 16-inch screen?

We won't know until the first Q2 deliveries arrive and we see what's been shipped.

@Mr MiserableOn a break

Ran the ship (pardon the pun) from the days his first Tesla was built and shipped from the USA. Now taking a well-earned break.

His story

How he came to run the shipping forum is a classic case of "if you want it done right, do it yourself".

  • The personal quest: like many early Tesla owners, he was frustrated by the lack of news about his own delivery. So he started digging into maritime data to find the ship carrying his car.
  • The method: he cross-referenced VIN registrations, port schedules at Pier 80 in San Francisco, and satellite AIS tracking of car carriers.
  • The name: legend has it his username reflects the miserable experience of waiting for a Tesla with no updates. By sharing what he found on TMC, he went from frustrated customer to the main source of information for thousands of others.
  • Taking the helm: his tracking became so accurate, often beating Tesla's own sales advisers on delivery dates, that he was asked to lead and moderate the Shipping Movements threads.

What he gave the community

  • The shipping spreadsheet: detailed tracking of every ship from Pier 80 to Zeebrugge or Oslo.
  • Ship-spotting guides: he taught people to use the Panama Canal webcams to watch their cars pass through the locks.
  • The support group: he turned the miserable wait into a shared hobby, where waiting became an investigative sport.
